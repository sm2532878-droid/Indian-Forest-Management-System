Endangered Species of India

Species facing high risk of extinction across Indian ecosystems

India is home to a vast range of endangered species due to habitat loss, climate change, poaching, pollution, and human-wildlife conflict. Conservation status is primarily assessed by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN).

Species classified as Endangered (EN) or Critically Endangered (CR) face a very high risk of extinction if conservation measures are not strengthened.

Bengal Tiger

Status: Endangered

Threats: Poaching, habitat loss

Latest Info: India recorded 3,167 tigers (2022 census)

Asiatic Lion

Status: Endangered

Threats: Limited habitat, disease risk

Range: Gir Forest, Gujarat

Indian Elephant

Status: Endangered

Threats: Habitat fragmentation

Latest: Human-elephant conflict rising

Snow Leopard

Status: Vulnerable

Threats: Climate change

Region: Himalayan belt

Red Panda

Status: Endangered

Threats: Habitat destruction, climate change

Region: Sikkim & Arunachal Pradesh

Gangetic River Dolphin

Status: Endangered

Threats: Water pollution, fishing nets

Region: Ganga & Brahmaputra Rivers

Indian Pangolin

Status: Endangered

Threats: Illegal wildlife trade

Region: Across India

Lion-tailed Macaque

Status: Endangered

Threats: Deforestation

Region: Western Ghats

Nilgiri Tahr

Status: Endangered

Threats: Habitat fragmentation

Region: Nilgiri Hills

Hoolock Gibbon

Status: Endangered

Threats: Forest loss & hunting

Region: Northeast India

Critically Endangered Birds & Reptiles

  • Great Indian Bustard (CR) – Threatened by power lines
  • White-rumped Vulture (CR) – Decline due to diclofenac
  • Gharial (CR) – River pollution & sand mining
  • Hawksbill Turtle (CR) – Marine pollution

Endangered Marine Species

  • Gangetic River Dolphin (EN) – India’s National Aquatic Animal
  • Dugong (VU) – Seagrass loss
  • Whale Shark (EN) – Overfishing
