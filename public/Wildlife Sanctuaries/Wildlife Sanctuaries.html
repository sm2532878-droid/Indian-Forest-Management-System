Wildlife Sanctuaries of India

Comprehensive information on protected wildlife habitats across India

Introduction

Wildlife Sanctuaries in India are protected areas established to conserve wildlife species and their natural habitats. Unlike National Parks, sanctuaries allow limited human activities under regulation while ensuring the protection of animals and ecosystems.

India has more than 560 Wildlife Sanctuaries, spread across forests, wetlands, grasslands, deserts, coastal and marine regions.

Objectives of Wildlife Sanctuaries

  • Protection of endangered and threatened species
  • Conservation of biodiversity and habitats
  • Support for wildlife research and monitoring
  • Promotion of eco-tourism and awareness
  • Reducing human–wildlife conflict

Major Wildlife Sanctuaries of India

Periyar Wildlife Sanctuary – Kerala

Famous For: Elephants, Tigers

Periyar Wildlife Sanctuary is one of the most well-managed sanctuaries in India, located in the Western Ghats. It is known for its scenic lake, elephant herds, and rich biodiversity.

Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary – Kerala

Famous For: Elephants, Leopards

Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary forms an important part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve and acts as a corridor for elephants and other large mammals.

Mudumalai Wildlife Sanctuary – Tamil Nadu

Famous For: Tigers, Elephants

Mudumalai Wildlife Sanctuary is one of the first sanctuaries in India and is part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, known for dense forests and rich wildlife diversity.

Bhitarkanika Wildlife Sanctuary – Odisha

Famous For: Saltwater Crocodiles

Bhitarkanika is a mangrove ecosystem sanctuary and supports one of the largest populations of saltwater crocodiles in India.

Chinnar Wildlife Sanctuary – Kerala

Famous For: Grizzled Giant Squirrel

Chinnar Wildlife Sanctuary is known for its dry deciduous forests and unique species, including the endangered grizzled giant squirrel.

Nalsarovar Bird Sanctuary – Gujarat

Famous For: Migratory Birds

Nalsarovar is a major wetland bird sanctuary and hosts thousands of migratory birds during the winter season.

Difference Between National Parks and Wildlife Sanctuaries

  • Sanctuaries allow limited human activity; National Parks do not
  • Sanctuaries focus on species protection; Parks focus on ecosystems
  • Sanctuaries may include private land; Parks do not

Complete Coverage of Wildlife Sanctuaries (State-wise)

Below is a summarized state-wise list covering wildlife sanctuaries across India.

  • Kerala: Periyar, Wayanad, Chinnar, Silent Valley
  • Tamil Nadu: Mudumalai, Sathyamangalam, Point Calimere
  • Karnataka: Bhadra, Dandeli, Cauvery
  • Madhya Pradesh: Kuno, Nauradehi, Gandhi Sagar
  • Rajasthan: Tal Chhapar, Mount Abu
  • Gujarat: Nalsarovar, Wild Ass Sanctuary
  • Odisha: Bhitarkanika, Satkosia
  • West Bengal: Buxa, Mahananda, Raiganj
  • Uttarakhand: Binsar, Kedarnath, Askot
  • Maharashtra: Tadoba buffer sanctuaries, Bhimashankar
  • Andaman & Nicobar: Interview Island, North Reef
  • Others: Pobitora, Hollongapar, Dachigam, Ranganathittu

Importance of Wildlife Sanctuaries

  • Conservation of endangered species
  • Maintaining ecological balance
  • Supporting wildlife corridors
  • Environmental education & tourism
  • Climate resilience and habitat protection
